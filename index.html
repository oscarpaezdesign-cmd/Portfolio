Circular railing for a grand double staircase

A custom ornamental steel railing system for a high-end residential double staircase, built from a single reference photo — precision layout across angled stair runs, intermediate landings, and curved balcony panels.

Role
Lead concept design
Scope
Concept design
Materials
Steel
Year
2023
Concept design by Oscar Páez · Fabrication: Artesano Iron Works

The challenge

The only starting point was a single reference image. From it, the project had to interpret the existing architecture and translate intricate decorative ironwork into precise production drawings — adapting the scrollwork to the staircase's own geometry, a 32.5° diagonal run, so the pattern's rhythm stayed intact across the flat landings.

What I did

Developed the full concept package: layout drawings for the angled runs and landings, scrollwork distribution calculated to the staircase's exact slope, and horizontal balcony panels that carry the same pattern language around the curve — balancing classical detail with the structural standards the fabrication team needed to build from.

Symphony in Bronze

A helical staircase railing in cast bronze, built around a tight 11'-7" curvature — over 20 individually cast ornamental details, from acanthus motifs to scroll flowers, set against warm mahogany paneling.

Role
Lead concept design
Scope
Concept design
Materials
Cast bronze
Year
2023
Concept design by Oscar Páez · Fabrication: Artesano Iron Works

The challenge

Translating neoclassical ornamentation into a multi-plane, helical structure meant developing an unfolded spiral geometry while keeping every scroll, cast leaf, and floral accent in absolute symmetry along a curve that left almost no room for error. More than 20 design revisions were needed to hold that tolerance.

What I did

Bridged concept and fabrication directly — converting the architectural constraints into precise 2D and 3D shop drawings for the spiral and the flat balcony runs, and specifying every custom-cast bronze component so it would align exactly with the mounting hardware and the existing woodwork on site.

22-foot ironwork entry system

A monumental entry door and curved transom window wall, conceptualized to anchor a residential facade — organic scrollwork carried across three tiers, engineered to hold oversized insulated glass at full height.

Role
Lead concept design
Scope
Concept design
Materials
Steel & insulated glass
Year
2024
Concept design by Oscar Páez · Fabrication: Artesano Iron Works

The challenge

At nearly 22 feet tall, the system needed to balance a massive vertical volume with delicate scrollwork without losing structural rhythm — dividing the facade into three proportional tiers so the entryway still reads at human scale while drawing the eye up through the glass.

What I did

Explored four distinct design proposals for the tiering and transom curves, then produced complete CAD documentation — scroll geometries, structural tube framing, and custom glass-retention profiles engineered specifically for the oversized insulated units each tier had to carry.

Corten steel architectural water feature

A monolithic water wall for a corporate headquarters garden, exploring the rich patina and organic durability of Corten steel — a louvered facade engineered to carry a continuous, even sheet of water.

Role
Lead concept design
Scope
Concept design
Materials
Corten steel
Year
2022
Concept design by Oscar Páez · Fabrication: Artesano Iron Works

The challenge

The core engineering problem sat in the manifold and reservoir: delivering perfectly even water distribution across every panel tier so the sheet fell smoothly, without splashing or dry patches, purely through calculated basin capacity and pressure head.

What I did

Designed the cascading Corten slat facade — a terraced profile that catches light and guides the water down — and split the structure into three transportable modules (distribution box, panel wall, reservoir basin) for straightforward installation and ongoing maintenance access.

How I work

Every project begins with understanding — the space, the people, and the purpose behind it — before a single line gets drawn. Good design is clear, intentional, and built to last, and every decision, structural or visual, should move toward a solution that's both elegant and buildable.

Most of what lands on my desk starts as one reference photo, a rough sketch, or a set of site dimensions — never a finished brief. Fifteen years of turning that kind of input into concepts a fabrication team can actually build from is the core of how I work now, whether the project is a piece of architectural ironwork or a brand identity.
